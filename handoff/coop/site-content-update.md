## About
I'm a sophomore at Northeastern studying Data Science and Business Administration, with concentrations in Supply Chain Management and International Business. Most of what I actually know, I learned by building something and then working out why it broke.

In January, I'm joining Verisk as a Technology Services Group Co-op, supporting the catastrophe-modeling software insurers use to understand disaster risk.

The pattern in my work is taking data that arrives messy and turning it into something a person can act on. For my DS 2500 capstone, my team pulled roughly 35,000 consumer complaints from the CFPB API to test whether federal enforcement actions actually change how large banks behave. Handling paginated JSON and inconsistent fields was most of the work. The analysis was the easy part.

That project pointed me at what I keep coming back to: the interesting problems live between the data and the decision. My combined major is deliberate. I want to work where analysis changes what a business actually does next, whether that's a bank, a retailer, or a supply chain.

I like the planning side too. For a project management capstone in Athens, I built a 195-day, 55-task rollout plan for a health monitoring system across a healthcare network, with a work breakdown structure, schedule, stakeholder map, and ten ranked risks. As Junior Technology Consultant at the Family Business Consulting Club, I own the club's website and support consulting teams on pro bono engagements with family-owned businesses. As webmaster for INFORMS at Northeastern, I run our chapter's website.

Outside of coursework I ship things. CardEdge is a poker and blackjack trainer that runs Monte Carlo equity simulations in cancellable Web Workers, reports 95% confidence intervals, stores training history in Postgres with row-level security, and checks test coverage on every push. IG Wrapped analyzes an Instagram data export entirely inside your browser, because I wanted the analysis without handing my data to anyone, and its AI analyst chat answers questions from aggregated metrics only. Both are live, and the code for both is public on GitHub.

This semester I'm taking Introduction to Databases, Advanced Programming with Data, and Mathematical Foundations of AI, which is turning the SQL I'd been using through Supabase into something more rigorous.

After my co-op ends in June, I'm looking for a summer internship starting in July 2027 in data science, analytics, or operations.
